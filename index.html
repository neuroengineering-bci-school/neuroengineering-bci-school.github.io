About the School

Bridging Neuroscience and Engineering in Latin America

A five-day intensive training school for researchers across Latin America and beyond, covering the full arc of neuroengineering from brain signals to working BCI systems. Open to MSc and PhD students, postdocs, and early-career researchers.

Travel support is being sought through IBRO funding for participants who need financial assistance to attend.

Why Attend
  • Hands-on experience with real EEG and BCI equipment
  • International faculty, active researchers in the field
  • Networking across Latin America
  • Present your own research
  • Poster session — showcase your work and get feedback from international faculty
  • Certificate of completion and course materials
  • Open to applicants from all across LATAM
Scientific Program

Five days. One complete pipeline.

From neural oscillations to clinical translation — each day builds systematically on the last.

Monday · The Brain as a Signal Source
Establishing the neuroscientific and clinical foundations of brain-computer interfaces
9:00 – 9:30
Ceremony

Opening Session

Welcome, faculty introductions, school objectives, and participant overview

9:30 – 10:20
Lecture

Neural Oscillations and Brain Rhythms

How populations of neurons synchronize to produce measurable electrical rhythms; frequency bands and their functional significance

10:20 – 11:00
Lecture

EEG Acquisition and Signal Fundamentals

Electrode placement, the 10-20 system, reference electrodes, signal propagation through the scalp, and common artifact sources

11:00 – 11:20
Coffee Break
11:20 – 12:10
Lecture

Neurological Disorders and the Rationale for BCIs

Motor neuron disease, spinal cord injury, stroke, and Parkinson's disease viewed through the lens of lost neural communication and what technology can restore

12:10 – 13:00
Lecture

Introduction to Brain-Computer Interfaces

Overview of BCI paradigms: P300, SSVEP, and motor imagery. The complete BCI pipeline from signal acquisition to device control

13:00 – 14:00
Lunch
14:00 – 15:00
Workshop

EEG Acquisition Workshop 1 — Equipment and Basics

Introduction to the EEG hardware: amplifier, electrodes, and cap; impedance checks, cap fitting, and electrode placement using the 10-20 system

15:00 – 16:00
Workshop

EEG Acquisition Workshop 2 — Live Recording

Participants set up electrodes on one another, record live EEG, inspect real-time signals, and identify common artifacts in the data

16:00 – 16:20
Coffee Break
16:20 – 17:00
Workshop

EEG Acquisition Workshop 2 — continued

Teams compare recordings, troubleshoot artifact sources, and discuss signal quality strategies with faculty guidance

Tuesday · From EEG Signals to BCI Paradigms
Acquiring the tools to transform raw EEG into interpretable BCI-ready data
9:00 – 9:50
Lecture

Sensorimotor Rhythms and Motor Imagery

Mu and beta ERD/ERS as the neural signature of motor intention; how imagining a movement activates the motor cortex and what this means for rehabilitation-oriented BCIs

9:50 – 11:00
Lecture

Event-Related Potentials

Overview of ERP components and their cognitive correlates: P300, N200, N400, and error-related negativity. Stimulus protocols, averaging strategies, and how ERPs are used across different BCI paradigms

11:00 – 11:20
Coffee Break
11:20 – 12:10
Lecture

Motor Imagery BCIs: Principles and Applications

Sensorimotor BCI design: spatial filtering with CSP, feedback paradigms, and clinical applications in motor rehabilitation and assistive technology

12:10 – 13:00
Lecture

Hybrid BCIs: Combining EEG with Other Signals

Why single-modality systems have limitations; combining EEG with EMG, fNIRS, and wearable sensors for orthosis and exoskeleton control; review of clinical deployments

13:00 – 14:00
Lunch
14:00 – 16:00
Workshop

BCI Based on P300 — Data Recording Workshop 1

Introduction to the P300 BCI paradigm software; participants learn the recording protocol, practice as subjects, and collect a first labeled EEG dataset together

16:00 – 16:20
Coffee Break
16:20 – 17:00
Workshop

BCI Based on P300 — Data Recording Workshop 2

Participants take full control: each person designs their own recording session, collects their own P300 dataset, and labels the data for later classification

Wednesday · EEG Signal Processing + Social Activity
Building the preprocessing and analysis toolkit — morning technical session, afternoon social program
9:00 – 9:50
Lecture

Time-Frequency Analysis and EEG Feature Extraction

Short-time Fourier transform, wavelets, and Hilbert transform for EEG; band-power features, spectral entropy, and connectivity measures used in BCI systems

9:50 – 11:00
Lecture

Functional Connectivity and Cortical Networks

Coherence, phase-locking, and graph-theory approaches to understanding how brain regions communicate — and what disrupted connectivity reveals about neurological conditions

11:00 – 11:20
Coffee Break
11:20 – 12:10
Workshop

EEG Processing Workshop 1 — MNE-Python Pipeline

MNE-Python pipeline using datasets from Monday and Tuesday: filtering, ICA, ERP extraction, and band-power computation

12:10 – 13:00
Workshop

EEG Processing Workshop 2 — Analysis and Comparison

Participants complete and compare preprocessing pipelines; group discussion of results, analytical challenges, and best practices

13:00 – 14:00
Lunch
14:00 – 18:00
Social Activity

Social Activity

Group social activity for participants and faculty — details to be announced

Thursday · Teaching Machines to Read the Brain
Machine learning, passive BCIs, open science, and participant poster presentations
9:00 – 9:50
Lecture

Machine Learning Foundations for EEG Decoding

Classical approaches: LDA, SVM, and CSP for motor imagery classification. Feature engineering for EEG: covariance matrices, band-power vectors, and spatial filters

9:50 – 11:00
Lecture

Deep Learning for EEG: CNNs and EEGNet

Convolutional neural networks adapted for EEG; EEGNet architecture; inter-subject variability, transfer learning, and model generalization across sessions

11:00 – 11:20
Coffee Break
11:20 – 12:10
Lecture

Passive BCIs and Cognitive State Decoding

Monitoring fatigue, attentional load, and cognitive state without active user input; applications in safety-critical environments, education, and clinical assessment

12:10 – 13:00
Lecture

Open Science, Reproducibility, and Neuroethics

Public EEG datasets, version-controlled pipelines, pre-registration, and FAIR data principles. Neuro-rights and data ethics: who owns the signals from your brain?

13:00 – 14:00
Lunch
14:00 – 16:00
Workshop

EEG Classification with ML — Workshop 1

End-to-end Python pipeline: loading participants' own recorded data, feature extraction, training SVM and CSP classifiers, and cross-validation

16:00 – 16:20
Coffee Break
16:20 – 16:40
Poster

Poster Setup

Participants hang their research posters in the session hall in preparation for Friday's poster session

16:40 – 17:00
Mini-project

Mini-Project Briefing and Team Formation

Faculty present Friday mini-project guidelines; teams form and select their analytical focus and dataset to prepare overnight

Friday · From Lab to Life
Clinical translation, mini-project showcases, poster sessions, and closing ceremony
9:00 – 9:50
Lecture

EEG-Based Neurofeedback and Brain Synchronization

How the brain synchronizes neural networks across regions; real-time neurofeedback paradigms using EEG oscillations; applications in cognitive enhancement, rehabilitation, and closed-loop BCI systems

9:50 – 11:00
Lecture

BCIs in Neurorehabilitation

State of the art in BCI-assisted recovery for stroke, spinal cord injury, ALS, and Parkinson's disease; clinical evidence, current limitations, and the road ahead

11:00 – 11:20
Coffee Break
11:20 – 11:45
Professional Dev

Grants and Scientific Writing

Structuring a competitive research proposal; writing abstracts and manuscripts; navigating peer review. Funding landscape for neuroengineering in Latin America: CONAHCYT, IBRO, NIH Fogarty

11:45 – 12:10
Professional Dev

Career Paths in Neuroengineering

Research groups, graduate programs, and funding opportunities in Latin America and globally; panel discussion with faculty

12:10 – 12:40
Poster

Participant Poster Session — Round 1

First half of participants present their research posters; faculty and peers engage with the work and provide structured feedback

13:00 – 14:00
Lunch
14:00 – 14:30
Poster

Participant Poster Session — Round 2

Second half of participants present their research posters; faculty and peers vote for the best scientific contribution

14:30 – 15:30
Mini-project

Mini-Project: Integrated BCI Pipeline — Execution

Teams work on their integrated pipeline: EEG recording, preprocessing, feature extraction, and classification. Faculty available for guidance

15:30 – 16:00
Mini-project

Mini-Project: Integrated BCI Pipeline — Presentations

Each team presents their pipeline and results (8–10 min); structured feedback from faculty and peers on methodology, interpretation, and next steps

16:00 – 16:20
Coffee Break
16:20 – 17:00
Ceremony

Closing Ceremony and Best Poster Award

Certificate presentation, Best Poster Award announcement, closing remarks by Dr. Antelis, group photo, and open networking. Participants receive curated resources: datasets, code repositories, and reading lists
