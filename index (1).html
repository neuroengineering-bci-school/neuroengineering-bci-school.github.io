About the School

Bridging Neuroscience and Engineering in Latin America

This school grows out of a simple conviction: that Latin America has the talent, the curiosity, and the urgency to lead in neuroengineering — and that what many researchers in the region lack is not ability, but access to rigorous, hands-on training that connects neuroscience with engineering from the ground up.

Over five days in Guadalajara, participants will move through the entire arc of a brain-computer interface project. They will record real EEG signals from one another, learn to clean and analyze those signals with professional tools, operate a live BCI system, apply machine learning to neural data, and present their own research alongside peers from across the region.

The program is designed so that each day builds on the previous one. By Friday, participants will not only understand how BCI systems work — they will have built one themselves.

Why Attend

An experience designed for impact

From EEG acquisition to AI-based neural decoding, this school offers a unique end-to-end training in neuroengineering.

Real hands-on experience

You will record live EEG from other participants, run a full preprocessing pipeline in MNE-Python, operate a P300-based BCI, and see a tDCS demonstration on research-grade equipment. Nothing is simulated.

Faculty who work in the field

Every instructor is an active researcher. The lectures come from people who have faced the same technical and clinical challenges you will encounter in your own work.

A Latin American community

This school is designed to connect researchers across the region. Many participants will leave with collaborators, not just contacts — people working on the same problems from different angles.

Resources that last beyond the week

Participants receive a certificate of completion along with curated datasets, annotated code repositories, and reading lists that continue to be useful long after the school ends.

Travel support being sought

We are actively seeking funding to cover travel, accommodation, and meals for participants who need financial support to attend. We believe geography and economic background should not determine who gets access to this training.

Your research on display

Two poster sessions give every participant space to present their own work and receive substantive feedback from faculty and peers throughout the week.

How to Apply

Applications open · 2026

Who can apply?

  • MSc and PhD students in neuroscience, engineering, or related fields
  • Postdoctoral fellows and early-career researchers
  • Strong preference for applicants from Latin America and underrepresented regions
  • Basic programming experience in Python is recommended

IBRO Travel Grants Available

Thanks to IBRO Schools Support funding, travel grants are available to cover travel, accommodation, and meals for selected participants. Priority will be given to applicants from economically disadvantaged backgrounds and underrepresented regions. Financial constraints should never prevent talented researchers from accessing world-class training.

Application process

  • Create an applicant account on the IBRO Grants Portal
  • Complete the online application form including a motivation letter
  • Provide a brief CV and a summary of your current research
  • Indicate whether you are applying for an IBRO travel grant
  • Applications reviewed on a rolling basis — apply early
